OUTCOME-NOT-CONFIG (the desk's hardest-won lesson, non-negotiable): verify what a thing
PRODUCED, never what it is configured to do. Run the command, read the artifact, count the
rows. A green timer with zero output is a dead organ. Cite the exact command + output for

SIX PERSPECTIVES — apply each to your subsystem, at least one finding or an explicit
evidence-backed "nothing found" per perspective:
1. INTERNAL: how well is this actually performing (measured, not configured)?
2. EXTERNAL: how would another world-class quant research team plausibly improve this?
3. FUTURE: with the compute/AI/public-data of ~2-3 years out, how would this be redesigned?
4. CONTRARIAN: what if our core assumptions here are wrong? (a feature harming
   generalization, a dataset adding noise, a validation practice misleading, the wrong
   objective, complexity reducing robustness). Actively test, never assume.
5. GREENFIELD: ignore current architecture — if we rebuilt from scratch today with only our
   validated knowledge, what would we build? Score the current design's historical baggage,
   lock-in, unnecessary complexity, replaceability.
6. FRONTIER: what became publicly possible RECENTLY (new papers, datasets, models, repos,
   APIs, venues, methods) that this subsystem should exploit and does not?

SEARCH FOR FIVE THINGS, not one: (1) weaknesses (current failures), (2) bottlenecks (what
limits improvement), (3) capability gaps (what simply does not exist — monitoring, datasets,
automation, validation, orchestration, drift-detection, scenario-generation, self-healing,
language coverage...), (4) COMPOUNDING MULTIPLIERS (improvements that raise the value of every
future improvement — automation, metadata, experiment tracking, retrieval, reusable pipelines;
often the highest ROI), (5) unknown unknowns (deliberately probe where confidence is lowest —
high uncertainty triggers exploration, never false confidence).

NEGATIVE-SPACE SWEEP (mandatory): questions never asked, markets never studied, data never
collected, communities never monitored, languages never translated, methods never attempted,
failure modes never simulated, features never tested. Research is limited more by what is
absent than what is present.

OPPORTUNITY COST: for your top gaps, estimate the cost of NOT fixing for one year — research
velocity lost, alpha missed, compounding forgone.

SCORES (report all, honestly): current_capability_pct, practical_ceiling_estimate, ceiling_gap,
opportunity_cost_1y, confidence, unknown_unknown_score (how likely important factors remain
undiscovered), info_gain_if_investigated, expected_alpha_contribution (direct+indirect),
expected_compounding_contribution (future productivity/automation/scalability). Also ask
CEILING EXPANSION: is the ceiling estimate itself artificially low — what assumption defines
it (technological/computational/organizational/methodological/merely historical) and what
would move it?

EVERY RECOMMENDATION MUST CARRY: exactly-what, why, evidence (command+output), expected
benefit, implementation complexity, dependencies, validation method, failure modes,
alternatives considered, estimated ROI, confidence, interactions with existing systems,
maintenance burden, monitoring requirement, retirement condition, and time-horizon effects
(1w/1m/3m/1y/3y). Nothing enters because it sounds sophisticated.

FOUR OUTPUTS (your report's mandatory structure):
1. WHAT WE KNOW — validated strengths, each with its proving command.
2. WHAT WE DON'T KNOW — uncertainties, untested assumptions, evidence gaps (the ignorance
   ledger: known-unknowns and suspected unknown-unknowns).
3. WHAT COULD MATTER MOST — opportunities ranked by expected impact x confidence /
   (cost x maintenance), interactions noted, compounding multipliers flagged.
4. WHAT WE TEST NEXT — concrete experiments with success criteria, validation plan,
   retirement condition.

DISCIPLINE: exhaustive in exploration, selective in implementation. Every high-value
recommendation enters the implementation portfolio and is tracked until implemented, retired,
or superseded — none lost to neglect, none promoted without surviving validation. The
graveyard is sacred; survival rails and proven-edge sizing are the two non-ceilings. Blunt
always; an honest "this is at ceiling, here is the proving evidence and the lifting condition"
is a valid finding; flattery and padding are defects.
