BUT I OMITTED THE GATE THAT KILLS THEM: NET OF COST. Applying it:
  USDC/USDT  -- 1.1bps spread vs 4-10bps round-trip fees. You pay ~5x the edge to capture it. DEAD.
  FDUSD/USDT -- persistent LEVEL (-15bps), not reversion to zero; it reverts TO -15bps, so only
                the 9bps wiggle is harvestable = marginal vs fees. Capturing the level requires

  stETH/ETH  -- half-life 0.3d (~7h) demands intraday execution the desk does not have.
  mSOL/SOL   -- sd 539bps on a ratio that should track within ~100bps is IMPLAUSIBLE. This is a
                DATA ARTIFACT (CoinGecko daily USD legs sampled non-synchronously), same class as
                the COOKIEUSDT 59bps-flat cost entry. Measuring my pipeline, not the market.

THE REAL LESSON -- why kimchi is rare:
  kimchi runs at ~142bps std, roughly 100x the USDC peg spread. Regional premiums are not
  conceptually special; they are simply BIG ENOUGH TO PAY FOR THEMSELVES. Stablecoin pegs and
  liquid-staking discounts are too tightly arbitraged precisely because their constraints are
  SOFT -- redemption is annoying not impossible, exit queues are days not months.

REFINED SEARCH CRITERION for future spread hunts (the structural test alone is necessary but
nowhere near sufficient):
    spread std > ~50bps  AND  a named PHYSICAL constraint  AND  free daily series
  This rules out pegs and LSDs on arrival. Large structural spreads survive where constraints are
  HARD: capital controls (KR/CN -- both already live), regulatory segmentation (CME vs offshore --
  cme_anchored_basis_dislocation is QUEUED AND UNTESTED, now the top candidate), and restricted
  markets (which the s13 legitimacy gate must rule on first).
