Principal correctly reframed data/moat (4.4GB, 11,980 hourly files, 30 symbols x spot/fut) as a

WHAT PHASE 1 ESTABLISHED (trustworthy):
  * data is WELL-FORMED: bad=0, crossed books=0 across all 60 symbol-sides after the parser fix
  * coverage is good: 120-153 hourly files per symbol, 2,000-2,400 depth records sampled each
  * median quality 75 (was 55 before the fix)

TWO PARSER DEFECTS IN MY OWN AUDIT, both caught, both material:
  1. The moat is a MIXED stream: k="d" depth records (~1,358/file) interleaved with k="t" TRADE
     records (~11,107/file, ~8x more numerous). My first pass read the first 250 LINES of each
     file -- almost entirely trades -- and counted them as corrupt books. It produced bad-row
     counts in the thousands, 82-99% "stale", median quality 55, and a verdict that the only
     proprietary dataset the desk owns was unusable. IT IS NOT. The parser was.
  2. STILL UNRESOLVED AND MORE IMPORTANT: these are DELTA records. A delta carries only the levels
     that CHANGED. Therefore:
       - "stale top-of-book" is normal delta behaviour, not staleness. TRX at 96.9% simply means
         TRX's top rarely moves -- which is TRUE, and not a defect.
       - depth-within-1%-of-touch computed from a SINGLE delta is a FRAGMENT, not a book.
     So depth_cv = 0.15 is meaningless, and the audit's headline conclusion -- "depth barely
     varies, regime clustering would be fitting noise" -- IS UNSUPPORTED. Do not act on it.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY REQUIRED BEFORE ANY REGIME WORK: BOOK RECONSTRUCTION -- apply deltas sequentially
from a snapshot to maintain a live ladder, then sample the RECONSTRUCTED book on a fixed clock.
Only then are depth, spread, imbalance and replenishment measurable. Until that exists, every
liquidity-state feature in the principal's Family A/B/C/D is unmeasurable, and any clustering would
be clustering fragments.
