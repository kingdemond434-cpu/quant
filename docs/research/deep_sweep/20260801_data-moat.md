Four independent faults, each sufficient on its own:

1. **The restore drill cannot fail on an EMPTY-BUT-PRESENT source — and that is the exact case

   `entry["sha256"]`, a digest returned by `_snapshot_sqlite()` *from that same replica* seconds
   earlier (`:151`) — a tautology — then runs `PRAGMA integrity_check`, which validates **file
   structure, never content**, and returns `ok` on a zero-table database. Nothing compares the
   replica to the *source*.
   **In fairness, and it changes the recommendation: this is not sloppy work.** The test suite is
   real — `test_corrupted_replica_fails_the_drill` exists and passes, and
   `test_backup_replicates_verifies_and_drills` does a genuine content check

   covered: replicated, absent, corrupted. **The uncovered boundary is "present but empty"** — it
   passes `src.exists()`, snapshots to a structurally-valid 4096-byte file, satisfies the hash
   tautology and satisfies `integrity_check`. One missing case out of four, and it is the one
   production hit.
2. **It certified a 0-table replica of a 22-table store.** `"bytes": 0` in the manifest is
   `_du(src)` — the source was zero bytes at that run. So the organ replicated nothing, called it
   `REPLICATED`, and passed its own drill.

## 6. SIX PERSPECTIVES

**1. INTERNAL (measured, not configured).** The moat's *collection* is healthy (7.2 GB, live at
00:26 during the audit) and its *memory content* is genuinely good (K1, K2, K6). Everything
between them is where it fails: 0.9% construction coverage, 6,000 of 27,000 tokens indexed, 5 of
9 features never screened, 63% of the lake unread, 50k liquidation rows unread. **The moat is
acquisition-rich and conversion-poor** — exactly the L1.28b shape, on data instead of findings.

**2. EXTERNAL — the motive-similar tier-1 cohort.** Three transferable practices, each concrete:
- **Jane Street / HRT / Optiver — the execution tape is the crown jewel and is *complete*.** Every
  fill, every partial, every reject, every cancel, tagged and immutable. Ours records completed
  pairs only (DM-12: no `open_fail` event type in 523 rows) and carries TCA fields on **10 of 523
  rows (1.9%)**. Their ERM is trained on the tail; ours excludes it by construction. This is the
  single largest cohort gap and it is not a technology gap.
- **RenTech/Medallion — the standing exception and the ceiling exemplar.** The documented core of
  that operation is a single decades-deep cleaned data store that *every* researcher queries, with
  the cleaning itself treated as the proprietary asset. Ours is split across three same-schema
  sqlites plus a phantom path (DM-2, now 5 readers), with the richest store outside the retrieval
  index (DM8-4). The transferable practice at our capacity band is not their scale — it is their
  **single canonical store with one access path**, which costs a day and is item M1/M2 territory.
- **Negative exemplars (the control group).** Which of our rails would have caught Alameda's
  failure — an internal record that did not reflect reality? Our answer should be the execution
  tape and the NAV attestation chain. The tape currently **misses one-legged fills entirely**
  (DM-12, a live catch at 01:34 yesterday) and the desk has already had two sources of truth for
  equity (the 13,155/4,500 split). The rail exists; it does not yet fire on the case that matters.
  **Tier grade movement:** the data-moat's "own execution record" line should be marked **down**
  from whatever it holds — completeness is 1.9% on TCA fields and 0% on failed attempts.

**3. FUTURE (2–3 years out).** The desk holds 7.2 GB of L2 and computes 6 hand-specified features
from it. With 2028 compute the obvious redesign is a **learned market-state representation** —
self-supervised over book snapshots, with nearest-neighbour analog retrieval ("show me the 20 most
similar hours in our history and what happened next"). Today that is impossible here: no torch, no
faiss, no `.npy` anywhere, no fitting step in any "embedding" module (DM8-8). The *cheap* version
is available now and is not blocked by compute — a k-NN over the 6 existing book features would
be a day's work and would make the 7.2 GB queryable as *experience* rather than as *storage*.

**4. CONTRARIAN — I tested three of our own assumptions and two failed.**
- *"The moat is the raw L2 stream."* **CONFIRMED FALSE this sweep (§5)** — and it is the single
  most valuable thing this audit did. Bybit republishes the stream free at 8× depth and 41×
  resolution, ~345 days deep. Under L1.11 the moat is explicitly the *transformation pipeline,
  never the raw dataset* — the constitution said so all along, and the desk's own universe map
  carried the refutation at `status: queued` while four consecutive sweeps wrote the opposite.
  **We were defending the wrong asset, and the right one (the transformations) is 0.9% built.**
  The lesson generalises past this stream: *every* "we have something rare" claim on this desk
  should carry the date its free-alternative search was last run. None currently does.
- *"Fusion adds value."* Already died honestly (07-31: all 5 combos negative). Scoping the negative
  result per the battery: the **ROUTE** failed (daily z-composite over 2–4 axes), not the
  **CAPABILITY** (fusion). No intraday fusion has ever been attempted, and the desk's highest-
  resolution data is intraday. The negative was correctly recorded and incorrectly generalised.
- *"The retrieval index is broken."* **False** — recall@1 is 100/100 (DM8-4). Two prior sweeps

**5. GREENFIELD.** Rebuilt today with only validated knowledge, the moat is: **one** sqlite (not
